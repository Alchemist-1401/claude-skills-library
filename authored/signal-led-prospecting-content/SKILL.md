# Signal-led prospecting and source-cited content

A free, manual-but-systematic version of the pattern behind paid "AI sales agent" ads: watch for buying signals, reach out only when the signal makes you relevant, and publish articles people (and search engines) can verify. Two plays. Neither sends, posts or publishes anything; the user approves and ships everything.

## Play A: signal-led prospecting

1. **Define the buyer in one sentence.** Who they are, what triggers a need, what they buy. If the user cannot say, stop here and work it out first; every later step keys off this sentence.
2. **Pick 3-5 free signal sources.** Examples: public job posts in the buyer's industry (hiring signals a project), launch and funding announcements, leadership changes, public community posts where people ask for recommendations (Reddit, X, LinkedIn, niche forums), Google Alerts on the problem phrase. No login-walled scraping, no paid databases.
3. **Review on a cadence.** A fixed slot, daily or weekly. For each signal record: what happened, who it is, why it maps to the buyer sentence, the date, and the source URL. Old signals are not signals; note the recency cutoff and stick to it.
4. **Qualify before drafting.** Fit plus timing, both required. If the signal does not explain why now, skip and say why.
5. **Research from public sources.** Company site, the person's public profile, their own recent posts. Two or three facts, each with a URL. Never invent context.
6. **Draft, don't send.** One short message that references the specific signal, says the relevant thing the user does, and asks one small question. In the user's voice (see the messaging guidance for grounding voice). The user reviews, edits and sends it themselves, on their own account, in their own time.
7. **Log the outcome.** Sent or skipped, reply or not, what was learned. The log is what makes the signals better next week.

## Play B: source-cited content

1. **Collect real buyer questions.** From sales conversations, support messages, community posts, the user's own search history. Ten honest questions beat fifty keyword guesses.
2. **Answer one question per article.** Direct answer in the first lines, then the detail. Question-shaped headings.
3. **Cite a checkable source for every factual claim.** Primary sources first: official docs, the study itself, the vendor's own pricing page, government pages. Link or name each one. Anything unverifiable is marked as the user's own experience or opinion, or left out.
4. **Add what only this business knows.** Real numbers, real examples, real opinions from the work. Generic rewritten content has no reason to be cited by anyone.
5. **Keep it current.** Put the checked date on time-sensitive claims and re-check them on a cadence.

No article is guaranteed to rank on Google or to be cited by ChatGPT or any other assistant; this play only controls being the most useful, most checkable answer to a real question.

## Rules

- Free only: no paid data providers, no paid outreach platforms, no paid SEO tools. Manual review and the user's own accounts cover the whole flow.
- Nothing is sent, posted, published or automated. Every message is a draft until the user ships it. Follow each platform's terms; no comment-gating, mass DMs or fake engagement.
- Facts are checked at the owning source, and the skill says what it could not verify.
- This is an original workflow, not a copy of any product. It does not pretend to replicate a paid service's proprietary data or agents.
- For multi-step outreach mechanics (sequences, follow-ups, call prep) once a conversation is warm, pair with the library's sales-team entry rather than duplicating it.
