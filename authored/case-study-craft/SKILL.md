# Case study craft

A case study is the highest-leverage proof asset a small business has, and most are wasted because they lead with metrics. The numbers are the supporting cast. The lead is the person: who they were before, what they doubted, and what changed. The reader should recognize themselves in the subject and feel their own objection get answered. That is the whole method; everything below is mechanics.

## Step 1: pick the subject by mirror, not by size

Choose the customer whose before-state matches the reader you want more of. A modest result for a relatable customer outsells a spectacular result for someone the reader cannot identify with. If the user wants AI-automation clients who doubt automation works, the best subject is a client who doubted it too, not the biggest logo.

## Step 2: intake interview

Get the material from the customer (or from the user's own records of the engagement). Ask:

1. What was going on before you bought? What did a bad week look like?
2. What had you already tried, and why did it fall short?
3. What almost stopped you from buying? What did you think this would be?
4. What was the turning point, the moment your mind changed?
5. What is different now, concretely? Numbers welcome, but describe the day-to-day change too.
6. What would you tell someone who is where you were?

Real answers only. Never invent, paraphrase into fiction, or "polish" a quote into something the customer did not say.

## Step 3: map objections to story moments

List the three to five objections the target reader brings ("too expensive", "won't work for my industry", "I can do this myself", "tried this before"). For each one, find the moment in the material that answers it: a before-state that matches the objection, a doubt the subject voiced, a turning point that resolved it. Every objection gets a moment; any objection with no moment is a gap to fill with another interview question, not with adjectives.

## Step 4: structure the draft

1. **Mirror opening.** Who the subject is, in words the target reader uses for themselves. One short paragraph.
2. **Before-state and mindset.** Their situation and their doubts, honestly, including doubts about the user's own offer. This is what makes the rest believable.
3. **The turning point.** What they decided and why. The objection answers live here, woven in, not as a list.
4. **After-state with evidence.** Concrete change first, then the numbers that support it. Every number needs a source the user can back up (their own records count; mark them as such).
5. **In their words.** One or two short verbatim quotes, used with the customer's permission.
6. **Soft close.** One line inviting readers who see themselves here, no pressure copy.

## Step 5: adapt per surface

- **Website or deck:** the full structure above.
- **Post or caption:** mirror opening, one objection, the turning point, one number, one quote.
- **Reel or video script:** the customer's before-moment as the hook, transformation as the payoff. Render and publish through the user's own tools and accounts; this skill writes words only.

## Rules

- Real, named-with-permission customers only. Anonymous or composite stories are labeled as such or not written at all.
- No invented quotes, numbers, logos or results. Unverifiable claims are cut, not hedged.
- Drafts go to the user for review, and the featured customer approves the final text before anything goes anywhere. This skill sends and publishes nothing.
- Keep the reader's mirror honest: do not select or distort facts to force a match.
