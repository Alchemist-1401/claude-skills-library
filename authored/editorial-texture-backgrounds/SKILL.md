# Editorial texture backgrounds

A background technique for motion graphics, paraphrased from a tutorial reel by @chrismoran__ (source at the end). The whole idea in one line: stop using photos or flat colors as backgrounds; build the background from a subtly tinted paper texture instead.

## Why

- A full-bleed photo background (the stock-looking building shot, the cityscape) reads as generic. It fights the foreground, dates the piece, and loses the editorial feel.
- A plain solid background, even a clean dark one, reads as unfinished. Something is missing.
- The editorial look comes from texture you barely notice: the viewer feels depth without being able to say why.

## The recipe

1. **Base:** a solid dark or neutral background. Black or near-black is the common choice; a muted gray works for lighter pieces.
2. **Texture layer:** place a paper texture on top of the base. Vintage newspaper or old paper scans are the classic choice. Rotate it so the columns do not sit square to the frame; a slight angle reads as laid-on-the-desk, not wallpaper. Free paper and print textures: TextureLab (texturelabs.org) is a well-known free source.
3. **Tint the texture down:** apply a tint / color-map effect over the texture layer (in After Effects this is Tint with "Map White To"; elsewhere, any duotone or colorize). Map the texture's whites to a dark tone close to the base color so the contrast stays subtle. You should see the texture when you look for it, not read it.
4. **Build the foreground editorially:** cut-out imagery (subjects isolated from their backgrounds), halftone or photocopy-style treatment on photos, and big editorial typography (high-contrast serif for headlines, small caps or mono for labels). Thin rules, dates, and marginalia complete the magazine feel.

## Checks before you call it done

- Squint test: the background texture should almost disappear. If you can read the newspaper, the tint is too hot.
- The photo subject, not the background, carries the color. Desaturate or halftone the background imagery; keep one accent color for type or a single element.
- Motion stays slow: a gentle drift or scale on the texture layer is enough. Editorial means printed-page calm, not parallax tricks.
- Light pieces can invert the recipe: cream or paper-white base, texture tinted toward the base, dark ink foreground.
