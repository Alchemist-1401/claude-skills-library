# Seven psychology rules for selling

Source: the Instagram reel "7 Psychology rules for selling anything to anyone" by @builtformoresis, https://www.instagram.com/reel/DcoEwqwRTcU/ . This is a paraphrase of the creator's rules of thumb. She cites no studies and her income claim is self-reported, so treat each rule as a hypothesis to test against real replies.

## Hypotheses

1. Emotion often leads and logic justifies. Try opening with the felt problem, then give reasons.
2. Escaping a pain may motivate more than gaining a benefit. Try naming the specific frustration the buyer would leave behind.
3. Clarity may work better than pressure. Say plainly who it is for and what changes.
4. Objections can be a doubt about fit, not just the stated reason. If a reply is ambiguous, ask a short, respectful question.
5. Urgency may come from the cost of staying the same. Use only real costs, never invented ones.
6. Calm, unneedy offers may land better than desperate ones. Make the offer and let them decide.
7. A price objection may be a value question. Check that the outcome is clear before changing price.

## Using it

- Writing: draft, then check which hypotheses the draft ignores.
- Reviewing: point to the smallest edit that applies one of them.
- Objections: take "too expensive", "not now" or "no" at face value. Only if it is unclear, ask one respectful question. Offer proof, a trial, or a guarantee only if it is real, available and authorized. Never make new promises.
- Follow-ups: honor any refusal or opt-out and the existing follow-up cadence. A "no" ends the pitch.

## Limits

- No manipulation, fake urgency, fake scarcity, or exaggerated pain. Every claim must be true.
- Sending to a prospect follows the owner's existing approval scope. Without a standing grant that covers it, get the owner's approval of the words first.
